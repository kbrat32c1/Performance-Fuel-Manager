# SPAR Rulebook

The single statement of how SPAR is meant to work. Kevin decides what goes in. One decision at a time; the app follows this document, not the other way around.

Every rule has three labels so an athlete or coach can tell them apart:

- **Intent and coaching experience:** what Kevin has observed and wants the system to do.
- **Research and guidance:** what has been studied or recommended, and in whom.
- **Unknown:** where neither gives a dependable answer for one athlete.

**Status** is one of: *draft* (proposed), *decided* (Kevin approved), *in the app* (built and checked).

---

## Principle

Plan ahead so as little as possible has to come off at the end. Staying hydrated supports performance, and water is the last lever for making weight. Making weight is never the whole goal.

---

## Rule 1: Late water loss and the recovery window

**Status:** draft

**Rule:** Plan ahead to minimize dehydration before weigh-in. Consider both the estimated fluid deficit and the athlete's expected time until their first match. Around 3% is an upper caution boundary in short-window guidance, not a target or a guarantee of recovery. A short window or signs of an existing deficit can warrant greater caution.

**In the app (planned):** Show the estimated fluid deficit and the time to that athlete's first match side by side, with the readings and assumptions behind the estimate. Don't combine them into one "safe" number. Never say an athlete is fully recovered because the scale came back up.

**The clock:** actual weigh-in time to the estimated time of that athlete's own first match. College windows run about 60 minutes to 2 hours and average about 1.5 hours, and depend on which weight class the dual or tournament starts at. Tournaments are less predictable. Show it as an estimate the athlete or coach can adjust.

**Intent and coaching experience:**
- Lose the last bit as water, and no more than about 3%, because more than that hurts performance.
- About 40 years of wrestling, coaching and cutting weight. *(Kevin to add specifics.)*

**Research and guidance:** *(Sources marked † were reported by a reviewer and not yet opened; check before citing.)*
- AIS making-weight guidance†: with under two hours to compete, don't exceed about 3% body-mass loss through dehydration.
- NATA position statement†: muscle function impaired around 3–4% in most studies; pooled effects above about 2%.
- Barley study†: 14 combat athletes, about 3.2% dehydration, three hours to recover: strength unchanged, 28% fewer reps in a muscle-endurance test.
- Small study of seven collegiate wrestlers†: no significant drop in a short cycling-power test after 3% loss and rehydration.
- Grappler's Guide (2005), pp. 81–89: performance breaks down around 2%; with about an hour, lose only 2–3 lb late.
- UFC Performance Institute Vol. 2 (2021), pp. 412–415, 432–435: assumes a weigh-in the morning before the fight, so it's a long-window source.

**Unknown:**
- How much a given athlete can drink, absorb and retain in 60–120 minutes. Intake rates aren't net rehydration rates.
- Whether body size changes that rate enough to matter.
- How a deficit from several days compares with one from the last workout.

## Decisions still to make, in order

1. **Final 24 hours:** how much should come off, by recovery window (same-day vs day-before).
2. **Water schedule for a short window:** today's app schedule, the UFC table, or the Grappler's Guide short-window plan. Includes when restriction starts, in hours before weigh-in, and sodium.
3. **Meet-day recovery:** fluid targets sized to the time available, and protein timing.
4. **Extreme Cut:** what it actually is. Its description (2–4 week fat-loss block) and its plan (weigh-in-week depletion) don't match.
5. **Sugar system vs low-carb guidance:** the UFC and Grappler's sources point toward lower carbs and more protein; the sugar system doesn't.

## Decided so far

- **Extreme vs Rapid Cut:** 12% is a preseason body-fat line (Extreme Cut); 7% over class on the scale is the in-season line (Rapid Cut). *In the app.*
- **No automatic "do not eat", extra-workout or water-cut commands.** Facts and "talk to your coach" instead. *In the app.*
- **Water loading is optional and off by default for new athletes.** *In the app.*
- **Coaching experience counts as evidence** and is labeled as such.
