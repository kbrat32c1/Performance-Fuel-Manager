# SPAR Rulebook

The single statement of how SPAR is meant to work. Kevin decides what goes in. One decision at a time; the app follows this document, not the other way around.

Every rule has three labels so an athlete or coach can tell them apart:

- **Intent and coaching experience:** what Kevin has observed and wants the system to do, plus what practitioners he learned from teach or report. Kept separate within the label.
- **Research and guidance:** what has been studied or recommended, and in whom.
- **Unknown:** where neither gives a dependable answer for one athlete.

**Status** is one of: *draft* (proposed), *decided* (Kevin approved), *in the app* (built and checked).

**About the numbers here.** Each one says where it came from. Numbers from research are comparison points from a specific group and setting. They are not limits, targets or predictions for one wrestler, and the app should not calculate from them unless a rule says so. Sources marked † were reported by a reviewer and not yet opened; check them before citing.

## Principles

*Status: draft*

1. **Plan ahead** so as little as possible has to come off at the end. Making weight is never the whole goal. The goal is making weight with the best chance to recover, compete and train all season.
2. **The order of what to lose:**
   1. **Fat**, over weeks, by planning ahead.
   2. **Gut contents**, in the last few days, without cutting fuel.
   3. **Muscle glycogen**, as little as possible, and only what the time available can restore.
   4. **Water**, last, starting hydrated.
3. **Hydrate either way.** Water loading is a separate, optional strategy.
4. **Signals, not clearances.** The app separates what was measured, what's estimated and how confident the estimate is. It never tells an athlete they're safe, hydrated or recovered.

## Rule 1: Late water loss and the recovery window

**Status:** draft

**Rule:** Plan ahead so the final fluid loss is as small as practical, and start that phase well hydrated. Consider both the estimated fluid deficit and the athlete's expected time until their first match. Use roughly 3% as an upper caution boundary, not a target. A short window or signs of an existing deficit can warrant greater caution. Some fluid can be replaced before competition, but full rehydration or unaffected performance isn't guaranteed.

**In the app (planned):** Show the estimated fluid deficit and the time to that athlete's first match side by side, with the readings and assumptions behind the estimate. Don't combine them into one "safe" number. Never say an athlete is fully recovered because the scale came back up.

**The clock:** actual weigh-in time to the estimated time of that athlete's own first match. College windows run about 60 minutes to 2 hours and average about 1.5 hours, and depend on which weight class the dual or tournament starts at. Tournaments are less predictable. Show it as an estimate the athlete or coach can adjust.

**Intent and coaching experience:**
- Why roughly 3%: the last day should be mostly water, that amount costs the least performance, and some of it can come back before the match.
- Start the final phase well hydrated. A 3% cut on top of an existing deficit is a bigger cut than it looks.
- About 40 years of wrestling, coaching and cutting weight. *(Kevin to add specifics.)*

**Research and guidance:**
- AIS making-weight guidance†: with under two hours to compete, don't exceed about 3% body-mass loss through dehydration.
- NATA position statement†: muscle function impaired around 3–4% in most studies; pooled effects above about 2%.
- Barley study†: 14 combat athletes, about 3.2% dehydration, three hours to recover: strength unchanged, 28% fewer reps in a muscle-endurance test.
- Small study of seven collegiate wrestlers†: no significant drop in a short cycling-power test after 3% loss and rehydration.
- Grappler's Guide (2005), pp. 81–89: performance breaks down around 2%; with about an hour, lose only 2–3 lb late.
- UFC Performance Institute Vol. 2 (2021), pp. 412–415, 432–435: assumes a weigh-in the morning before the fight, so it's a long-window source.

**Unknown:**
- How much a given athlete can drink, absorb and retain in 60–120 minutes. Intake rates aren't net rehydration rates.
- Whether body size changes that rate enough to matter.
- How a deficit from several days compares with one from the last workout.
- Whether an athlete really started hydrated. Morning weight, urine color and specific gravity are possible signals. None proves it alone, and hard practices or illness can leave someone short even when drinking normally.

## Rule 2: Protect muscle glycogen

**Status:** draft. **Kevin to decide** whether, before a short-window weigh-in, glycogen is protected or a modest drop is accepted.

**Rule:** Preserve muscle fuel as much as practical. Some glycogen may need to come off to make weight, but it's one of the last things to lose, and only as much as can be put back before the first match and between matches. Don't assume a fixed glycogen loss or full restoration from scale weight or carb intake alone.

**In the app (planned):** Never show a glycogen percentage, because the app can't measure it. Teach the order of what to lose, and flag when carbs have been low going into a short window.

**Intent and coaching experience:**
- Glycogen is stored fuel and holds water, so losing it drops weight, but it's what hard, repeated efforts run on.
- Lose only what can be put back. In a 60–120 minute window, that points toward protecting it.

**Research and guidance:**
- Wrestlers who lost 6% in 53 hours dropped muscle glycogen by 36.5%, and 7.1 g/kg of carbohydrate did not restore it in 13 hours ([Kondo 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8379859/), n=10). The study didn't test performance.
- Reviews† discuss concern once roughly 40–50% of a well-fueled store is gone, and a proposed threshold near 250–300 mmol/kg dry weight is cited secondhand. One study† found no detectable effect in short cycling tests at about 30% lower; another† found worse repeated sprints at lower stores. None is a wrestling cutoff.
- Glycogen holds at least about 3 g of water per gram ([Fernández-Elías 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25911631/), n=9). The ratio isn't fixed.
- UFC handbook, p. 412: glycogen as 2–4% of body weight in a cut, in a long-window setting.

**Unknown:**
- How much a wrestler can lose before match performance suffers, especially across several matches.
- How much refills in 60–120 minutes. Best-case rates in cyclists need about 1.2 g/kg/hour and were measured over about 4 hours. The rate's units and source need checking before anyone calculates a percentage restored.
- FINAL 13 says 70–80% restored in 4–6 hours and full restoration in about 24. That looks optimistic next to Kondo and needs checking. The local coach's note that glycogen returns "in 2 hours" should be corrected.

## Rule 3: Gut contents

**Status:** draft. **Kevin to decide** how long the low-fiber period runs.

**Rule:** Gut contents are potentially the lowest-cost weight to lose, when managed without compromising fueling. They're not guaranteed free weight. Don't skip meals or under-eat to do it. Gut weight doesn't have to be replaced as fluid after weigh-in.

**Intent and coaching experience:** Lose gut contents before glycogen or water.

**Research and guidance:**
- UFC handbook, pp. 412–413: gut content 1–2% of body weight; low fiber for two days starts emptying, four days at most.
- One small study†: 19 men, lower-fiber period with matched calories, about 1.3 lb lower by the next morning on average. Performance wasn't tested.
- Grappler's Guide, pp. 83, 86: the opposite approach, adding fiber to clear the gut. The rulebook should pick one; the app uses low fiber now.

**Unknown:** How much any one athlete loses, and when a long low-fiber stretch starts to cost fueling or digestion.

## Rule 4: Water loading

**Status:** draft. The opt-in and explanation are *in the app*. The schedule itself is decision 2 below.

**Rule:** Optional, off by default. Water loading may produce a modest extra weight loss once intake drops. It isn't a substitute for staying hydrated, and it only helps if fluid is then restricted. Don't use it to make up for being far over.

**Intent and coaching experience:** Water loading helps start the final phase hydrated and gives roughly one extra pound of loss.

**Research and guidance:**
- [Reale 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29182412/): 21 male combat athletes; loading group lost 0.6% more body mass, about 0.9 lb for a 157 on average. Electrolytes stayed in range and performance didn't differ after a rehydration period over two days.
- UFC handbook, p. 414: widespread in MMA and works "at least for some athletes"; low blood sodium from excess water can mean hospitalization or death.
- Cho and Han 2020†: 13 university wrestlers over two weeks; no significant difference between groups.

**Unknown:** Whether the app's schedule, weekly repetition, or a short recovery window give the same result. Electrolytes matter while drinking more, but they don't remove the risk of drinking too much.

**Schedules to compare, for a Saturday weigh-in:**

| Day | App now | UFC table (p. 414) | Grappler's short window (p. 84) | Grappler's long window (p. 85) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 days out | ~80 ml/kg, plus salt | Not part of it | Normal | 2–3 gal, extra salt |
| 4 days out | 100 ml/kg | 100 ml/kg | Normal | 2–3 gal, extra salt |
| 3 days out | 100 ml/kg | 100 ml/kg | 2–3 gal | 2–3 gal, extra salt |
| 2 days out | ~20 ml/kg | 100 ml/kg | About 1 gal | 1 gal, no extra salt |
| 1 day out | Sips | Varies | Very little | None until weigh-in |

Holding Thursday as a loading day would need a higher Friday target or a bigger final drop, which conflicts with roughly 3% on the last day. See decision 1.

## Rule 5: After weigh-in

**Status:** draft, except that food type doesn't depend on cut type (decided). **Kevin to decide** protein timing, sodium amount and between-match guidance.

**Rule:** Have familiar, easy-to-tolerate food and drinks ready before weigh-in, and start promptly. Prioritize fluid with sodium, and carbohydrate. Avoid large, high-fat or high-fiber meals before wrestling. Size targets to the time available. Weight coming back on the scale doesn't prove recovery.

**In the app now:** sip fluids with electrolytes right away, 30–50 g of carbs per hour. **Problem:** the total fluid target (16–24 oz per lb) doesn't change with time to first match, and exceeds what the page's own hourly rate delivers in a short window.

**Intent and coaching experience:** Fill back what was actually lost. Not all carbs are handled the same.

**Research and guidance:** *(comparison points only)*
- Fluid and sodium hold better than plain water: about 58% retained with water vs 74–77% with a sports drink or oral rehydration solution at 3.5 hours (Ly†).
- AIS†: 1.2–1.5× the estimated deficit over 2–4 hours. UFC handbook: 150–200% of the deficit, in a long window. These suggest fluid targets should depend on time.
- Glucose–fructose mixes can increase absorption and about double liver glycogen refilling, and may be easier on the gut at high intakes for some athletes ([Gonzalez 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5409683/)). Not faster for muscle glycogen when carbohydrate is ample.
- UFC handbook, pp. 432–434: early carbohydrate up to about 60 g/hour, about 1.5 g sodium per liter, only low to moderate protein later. Long-window context.
- Grappler's Guide, p. 84: don't slam food or drink; about 1.5 L/hour as a maximum.

**Unknown:** How much any wrestler can absorb, retain and restore in 60–120 minutes; the best carbohydrate and sodium amounts for that window; whether a small amount of protein matters either way.

## The sugar system and protein cycling

**Status:** Kevin's experience-based approach. Not decided as a rule.

**Hypothesis:** A periodized, high-carbohydrate plan with short low-protein periods and later refeeds can produce useful fat or temporary weight loss while maintaining muscle, training quality and competition performance across a wrestling season. Protein can be looked at weekly rather than daily, with a short deficit made up on a refeed day.

**Intent and coaching experience:**
- *Kevin's own:* "My experience is that it does." *(Details to come from the questions below.)*
- *Practitioner sources:* Joe Binley's *Fueled to Thrive* and others, adapted by Kevin into his hybrid. *(Kevin to note what he took and what he changed.)* Several people repeating a claim may be one source, not several.

**Research and guidance:**
- Fructose and glucose are handled differently, and the body converts fructose to glucose: about 41% over 3–6 hours on average ([Sun & Empie 2012](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3533803/)). The amount depends on circumstances.
- Pure fructose has usually refilled muscle glycogen more slowly than glucose, though mixed meals narrow the gap ([2017 trial](https://doc.rero.ch/record/331064/files/AJCN_105_3_609.pdf)). The app's "processed by the liver, so your muscles stay fueled" isn't established.
- FGF21 rises with lower protein and with fructose in human studies†. Fat loss, muscle preservation and wrestling performance from the full schedule haven't been shown.
- The UFC and Grappler's sources point toward lower carbohydrate and more protein while cutting.

**Unknown:**
- Whether a refeed makes up for low protein during a calorie deficit and hard training. Protein needs may go up in a deficit, which needs checking.
- Whether a two-day dip and a four-day one behave differently.
- Horswill 1990 (wrestlers, 6% loss in 4 days, high vs low carbohydrate) is directly relevant. Its results still need to be read.

**Questions for Kevin:**
1. Which version: how many low-protein days, and what was eaten on them?
2. Who did it: your own cuts, or wrestlers you coached? Roughly how many, and what weight classes?
3. How much came off, and how fast?
4. What happened to performance, in practice, late in matches and late in tournaments?
5. What did the refeed look like, and how long until they felt normal?
6. Over a season, did they hold strength and muscle?
7. When did it not work, and for whom?

**A way to get wrestling data:** body composition every 2–4 weeks measured the same way, a repeatable strength and repeat-effort test, and how athletes feel and perform across a season.

## Decided so far

- **Extreme vs Rapid Cut:** 12% is a preseason body-fat line (Extreme Cut); 7% over class on the scale is the in-season line (Rapid Cut). *In the app.*
- **No automatic "do not eat", extra-workout or water-cut commands.** Facts and "talk to your coach" instead. *In the app.*
- **Water loading is optional and off by default for new athletes.** *In the app.*
- **Coaching experience counts as evidence** and is labeled as such.
- **After weigh-in, food type doesn't depend on the cut type** (Kevin, 2026-11-08). One after-weigh-in plan for every protocol. *Not in the app yet:* the coach, Food and protocol text still differ by protocol, and the Rapid Cut line both gives post-weigh-in protein and forbids it until wrestling is over.
